# Evolution-public-goods-altruism
Scripts used for the manuscript The evolution of Public goods altruism

There are three scripts:
1) the appendix AP11. is a plotting function designed to plot multiple (matrix based) images on the same figure and just needs to be loaded at the beginning of the main script using the proper path.
2) the script 1_ is used to investigate the effect of local competition, including evaluating the accuracy of the analytical approximation derived in the main document. The first algorithm that searches the evolutionary equilibrium is fully detailed. All the other ones but the last two are variations of the first one, where either the level of investment I or the level of relatedness R_LT are varied. The last two algorithms are used to find numerically the thresholds for altruism monomorphism and altruist/cheater polymorphism. These thresholds (based on the exact solution) are then compared to the approximation derived analytically to show which variables impact its accuracy (and to confirm that the approximation is accurate for a wide range of the parameter space).
3) the script 2_ is used to illustrate two different phenomena: first, the asymmetry of the area invasion (above or below the ESS) in the case of a multiplicative linear trade-off (Fitness has the shape W=(1-cI)(1+bIp)); second, the influence of the shape of fitness functions on the potential evolutionary outcome. Except for the first plot, these phenomenon are investigated by drawing pairwise invasibility plots that distinghuish the area of mutual invasion (coral colour) and the area of fixation of mutant (blue colour). These plots are used in section SI Text 6 to show that a range of fitness functions lead to similar outcomes than the multiplicative trade-off shape, and to show how certain functions can lead to different outcomes. The script begins with the definition of the different fitness functions used, before explaining the algorithm behind PIPs in a simple case and then exploits that algorithm to highlight more complicated outcomes.
